Universe Simulator v5.4 - Help Guide

Program Overview

This simulator recreates 13.8 billion years of cosmic evolution from the Big Bang to the present. Experience quantum fluctuations, galaxy formation, star birth, supernovae, and the emergence of life in real time.

Basic Controls

  • Play: Start the simulation
  • Pause: Pause the simulation
  • Reset: Reset the universe to initial state
  • Mouse Drag: Pan the view
  • Mouse Wheel: Zoom in/out

Parameter Explanation

  • Gravity (G): Gravitational constant. Higher values accelerate galaxy clustering
  • Dark Energy (Λ): Force accelerating cosmic expansion. ~70% of real universe
  • Galaxies: Maximum number of galaxies to generate
  • Planets: Maximum number of planets to generate
  • Inflation: Initial rapid expansion rate (currently unused)
  • Speed: Simulation time multiplier

Visual Object Meanings

Galaxies (Spiral Structures)

  • Blue-white galaxy: Young, hot stars (~8000K)
  • Red galaxy: Old, cool stars (~4000K) + redshift effect
  • Yellow core: High-density central region
  • Each galaxy has an ID (e.g., G-001)

Planets

  • Blue planet: Normal planet without life
  • Green planet: Life has emerged! In habitable zone (100–250 units from galaxy center) with C, Si, etc.
  • Each planet has an ID (e.g., P-042)

Black Holes

  • Black center with red aura
  • ~50% chance from supernovae
  • Absorbs nearby planets (within 10 units)

Dark Matter

  • Translucent purple regions
  • Appears after recombination (~380,000 years), forms scaffold for galaxies

Background Stars

  • White dots: Modern universe (after 50% age)
  • Red dots: Early universe (high redshift)

Statistics Guide

  • Universe Age: Current simulation time (in billion years)
  • Planets/Galaxies/BH: Current counts
  • C/Fe: Carbon and iron — produced by fusion and supernovae
  • Temp: CMB temperature. Starts at 2.7×10¹⁵ K, cools to 2.725 K today

Major Cosmic Events

  • 10⁻¹² s: Quark era ends, protons/neutrons form
  • 1 s: Lepton era ends, electrons stabilize
  • 3 min: Big Bang nucleosynthesis ends, helium forms
  • 380,000 years: Recombination — CMB released, dark matter emerges
  • 400 million years: First stars (Population III)
  • 800 million+ years: Galaxy structuring begins
  • Every 5 billion years: Galaxy merger events
  • After 12.4 billion years: Life possible on habitable planets

Element Formation Process

  • Carbon (C): Starts with star formation. Foundation of life
  • Oxygen (O): Star formation. Essential for respiration
  • Nitrogen (N): Star formation. Key for atmosphere & proteins
  • Neon (Ne): After 1 billion years in stars
  • Magnesium (Mg): After 2 billion years. Light metal
  • Silicon (Si): After 4 billion years. Rocky planet material
  • Sulfur (S): After 4.5 billion years. Protein component
  • Iron (Fe): Supernova explosion (after 5 billion years). Most stable element
  • Gold (Au): Neutron star mergers (extremely rare)

Simulation Tips

  • Gravity (G): 0.5+ for galaxy formation, 0.5–1.5 ideal for life, 2.0+ → black holes dominate
  • Dark Energy (Λ): <2.0 for structure, >3.0 → no galaxies or life
  • Galaxy/Planet sliders: Limit max count. Too low → fewer life chances
  • Life requires 9.7+ billion years and balanced G/Λ
  • Timeline slider jumps to any era (going back resets)
  • Event log tracks cosmic history

Physical Background

Simplified implementation of:

  • Hubble's Law: Expansion velocity
  • Newtonian Gravity: Attraction between bodies
  • Stefan-Boltzmann Law: Temperature vs scale factor
  • Nucleosynthesis Theory: Element creation in stars/supernovae
