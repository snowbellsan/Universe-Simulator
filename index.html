🌌 Universe Simulator v5.4 - Help Guide

📖 Program Overview

This simulator reproduces the evolution of the universe over 13.8 billion years, from the Big Bang to the present day. Experience the epic history of the cosmos in real-time, starting from quantum fluctuations to galaxy formation, star birth, supernova explosions, and the eventual emergence of life.

🎮 Basic Controls

  • ▶️ Start: Starts the simulation
  • ⏸️ Pause: Pauses the simulation
  • 🔄 Reset: Returns the universe to its initial state
  • Mouse Drag: Moves the viewpoint (Pan operation)
  • Mouse Wheel: Zooms in/Zooms out

⚙️ Parameter Descriptions

  • Gravity (G): Gravitational constant. Higher values lead to stronger celestial attraction and faster galaxy condensation
  • Dark Energy (Λ): The force accelerating the universe's expansion. It accounts for about 70% of the real universe
  • Galaxy Count: The maximum number of galaxies to be generated
  • Planet Count: The maximum number of planets to be generated
  • Inflation: The rapid expansion rate of the early universe (currently unused)
  • Speed: Time multiplier for the simulation speed

🎨 Meaning of Displayed Objects

🌌 Galaxies (Spiral Structures)

  • Bluish-White Galaxy: Galaxies with many young, hot stars (approx. 8000K)
  • Red Galaxy: Galaxies composed mainly of old, cool stars (approx. 4000K). This also represents Redshift
  • Yellow Nucleus: The center of the galaxy where high-density stars gather
  • Each galaxy is assigned an ID (e.g., G-001)

🪐 Planets

  • Blue Planet: A normal planet where life has not yet emerged
  • Green Planet: A planet where Life has emerged! It forms in the habitable zone (100 to 250 units from the galactic center) when elements like Carbon (C) and Silicon (Si) are present
  • Each planet displays an ID (e.g., P-042)

🕳 Black Holes

  • A celestial body with a black center and a red aura
  • Generated in about 50% of supernova explosions
  • It absorbs nearby planets (within a radius of 10 units)

🌑 Dark Matter

  • A translucent, purplish region
  • Generated after the Recombination Epoch (380,000 years), serving as a "scaffolding" for galaxy formation
  • It is invisible but plays a crucial role in cosmic structure formation through gravity

⭐ Background Stars

  • White dots: Normal universe (after 50% of the universe's age)
  • Red dots: Early universe (era of prominent redshift)

📊 How to Read Statistics

  • Universe Age: Current simulation time (in billion years)
  • Planets/Galaxies/BH: Current count of each celestial body
  • C/Fe: Elemental abundance of Carbon and Iron. They are generated by stellar nucleosynthesis and supernova explosions
  • Temperature: Cosmic background radiation temperature. Initially super hot at $2.7 \times 10^{15} \text{ K}$, cooling to $2.725 \text{ K}$ today

🌟 Major Cosmic Events

  • $10^{-12}$ seconds: End of the Quark Epoch, formation of protons and neutrons
  • 1 second: End of the Lepton Epoch, electrons become stable
  • 3 minutes (180 seconds): Completion of Big Bang Nucleosynthesis, Helium is born
  • 380,000 years: 🌌 Recombination Epoch (Decoupling), CMB released, Dark Matter appears
  • 400 million years: ⭐ Cosmic Dawn, birth of the first stars (Population III stars)
  • 800 million years onwards: Formation of initial star clusters, start of galactic structuring
  • 5 Billion Year Cycle: 💥 Galaxy Merging Events occur
  • 12.4 Billion Years onwards: 🌱 Possibility of Life Emergence (Habitable Planets)

⚛️ Element Generation Process

  • Carbon (C): Generation begins at star birth. The foundational element for life
  • Oxygen (O): Generation begins at star birth. Essential for respiration
  • Nitrogen (N): Generation begins at star birth. Component of the atmosphere and proteins
  • Neon (Ne): Generated after 1 billion years of stellar age. Noble gas element
  • Magnesium (Mg): Generated after 2 billion years of stellar age. Light metal
  • Silicon (Si): Generated after 4 billion years of stellar age. Key material for rocky planets
  • Sulfur (S): Generated after 4.5 billion years of stellar age. Component of proteins
  • Iron (Fe): Released in large quantities during supernova explosions (after 5 billion years of stellar age). The most stable element
  • Gold (Au): Generated in neutron star merger events (extremely rare). Super-heavy element

🎯 Simulation Tips

  • Gravity (G): Galaxy formation starts above 0.5; 0.5 to 1.5 is optimal for life; above 2.0 everything collapses into black holes
  • Dark Energy (Λ): Structure formation is possible below 2.0; above 3.0, matter cannot condense, and neither galaxies nor life will emerge
  • Galaxy/Planet Count Sliders: Limits the maximum number generated. Too few reduces the chance of life emerging
  • To see life emerge, advance past 9.7 billion years, and have appropriate Gravity/Dark Energy values
  • The Timeline slider allows jumping to any era (going back resets the simulation)
  • You can track the universe's history in the Event Log

🔬 Physical Background

This simulator implements simplified versions of the following physical laws:

  • Hubble's Law: Recessional velocity of celestial bodies due to cosmic expansion
  • Newtonian Gravity: Gravitational interaction between celestial bodies
  • Stefan-Boltzmann Law: Relationship between temperature and the cosmic scale factor
  • Nucleosynthesis Theory: Stellar fusion and supernova element generation
